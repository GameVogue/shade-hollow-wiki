Shade-weaving is a broad term for the practice of utilizing the magical energy left behind by human souls when they die. Most weavers can only perform small tricks and the art has only been academically studied in the Kingdom for two generations, although religious figures have been practicing intuitively for hundreds of years.

### Shade-Alchemy

**A Shade-Weaver can, in order of difficulty:**
*The size of the effect also exponentially affects difficulty.*
- Create a light
- Create a sound
- Create heat or sparks
- Create fog or mist
- Render an object temporarily obscured or invisible
- Accelerate a natural process such as growth, healing, or decay.

The effect can last as long as there is energy remaining in the area and stamina remaining in the caster. Weaving can be quite tiring for some, and requires concentration.

A common weaver can illuminate their path on a dark night and light their campfire when it comes time to rest, and perhaps impress a friend by growing a flower from the ground if circumstances are right.

An exceptionally strong weaver might blind and deafen a rowdy taproom, heal a grievous flesh wound, render themselves invisible for an hour, or cause the arm of a man to age and shrivel if he placed a hand on them.

Many injuries are beyond a weaver if they are internal or complex in nature. Many objects, especially moving or living ones, are very difficult to render invisible.

### Theurgic Weavers
The Church sponsors the learning of shade-weaving in isolated monasteries, under the strict doctrine of the Church. These weavers often focus on healing, as it is the service that most commonly brings people into the church.

### Thaumaturgic Weavers
Several cities have new colleges or guilds dedicated to the study of shade-weaving, either for the sake of knowledge itself or for applications in profit. Weavers are now sometimes hired to accompany traders in caravans or ships. Others are sometimes hired by hospitals or used in various military or law-enforcement positions. All of these applications are largely experimental.

#### Hedge Weavers
Alongside folk religions, there are witches and druids who practice their own methods of shade weaving, which is largely the same as weaving anywhere else, but with different rituals attached. Hedge weavers were the first known weavers, and it is theorized that the earliest among them were taught by [[Shades]] themselves hundreds of years ago.
